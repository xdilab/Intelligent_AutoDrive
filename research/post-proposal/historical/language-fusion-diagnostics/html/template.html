01 / Main findings

The benefit is selective—and not reliably additive.

The strongest existing result is the standalone phrase head’s improvement over the flat classifier on rare triplets. A stronger composition model already captures much of that benefit. The remaining complementary predictions are the target of this diagnosis.

03 / Prediction-level tracing

Does Stage 6 keep the phrase head’s recoveries?

A phrase-only opportunity is a ground-truth instance recovered by the phrase head but missed by Stage 5 at the same prediction budget. We then check whether Stage 6 recovers that same instance.

How to read the budget: for each class, keep the top K predictions across all evaluation frames. The default K equals that class’s evaluation ground-truth count. This gives every model the same number of predictions for comparison. It uses ground truth for diagnosis; it is not a deployment threshold or a rule for fitting a gate.

Recovered by Stage 6Missed by Stage 6Percent of phrase-only opportunities at the selected budget.

Counts are GT instances per frame, not unique vehicles or tracks. “Mean” averages counts across three training runs; it does not triple the number of independent observations. The plotted mean percentage is the ratio of mean recovered counts to mean opportunities. At zero opportunities, retention is undefined.

The whole tradeoff: recoveries, losses, and false positives

Stage 6 can recover instances the phrase head misses, and can lose correct Stage 5 detections. At a fixed K, false positives equal K minus true positives. They include duplicates, localization errors, wrong-class predictions, and background.

Why these six classes?

Classes were selected from the existing AP table before examining individual detections. They deliberately include gains and regressions. This is a disclosed case study, not a random sample or an unbiased estimate of fusion behavior across all 86 classes.

05 / Interpretation

A measurable opportunity for selective fusion.

Supported by this analysis

  • The phrase head and Stage 5 make complementary predictions.
  • Stage 6 retains some phrase-only recoveries and misses others.
  • Effects vary across classes, seeds, and prediction budgets.
  • Some Stage 6 gains come from recoveries beyond the phrase-only set.
  • Matching the official AP results makes these traces auditable against the completed study.

Still an open question

  • Whether semantic information, classifier structure, or regularization explains a given benefit.
  • Whether a gate can reliably choose the better prediction without ground truth.
  • Whether feature-level conditioning outperforms score fusion.
  • Whether the patterns generalize to unexamined classes or datasets.
  • Which false positives reflect semantic confusion versus localization or duplicate matching.

Test whether a small gate, fitted on held-out training videos, can preserve useful complementary predictions on unseen evaluation data.

Compare Stage 5, the current Stage 6, a global blend, and a regularized class-specific gate first. Keep the original encoder and candidates fixed. A later input-dependent gate or feature-conditioning module should be a separate experiment. The final-validation examples in this report must not be used to train or select that gate.

The separate BDD-X adaptation study is not pooled into these diagnostics. Its final three-seed mean adaptation effects were +0.0298 triplet AP points for Stage 5 and −0.1559 for Stage 6; it did not establish an increased contribution from phrase evidence.

06 / Methods, checks, and source data

Reproducible from frozen checkpoints.

No retraining or video re-encoding was performed for this diagnosis. The original evaluation features and four model checkpoints per seed were reused. Only six selected output classes were traced, with the complete frame and candidate population retained.

Matching and ranking

  1. Apply the original score assembly and YOLO confidence exactly once.
  2. Within each frame and class, sort candidates by score and greedily match unused GT boxes at IoU ≥ 0.5.
  3. Sort all class predictions globally and compute the original VOC precision-envelope AP.
  4. Record each GT instance’s matched rank and candidate identity.
  5. Compare recovered GT sets at the same prediction budget.

Winning candidate boxes can differ across models. The example score table reports the same cyan box for every model, while GT rank refers to whichever box matched that GT in that model. Scores are not assumed to be calibrated across model families.

Limitations and sampling details
  • Six classes were chosen post-hoc from known AP gains and losses. Conclusions about their traces should not be generalized to all classes without extending the analysis.
  • The 86-class overview uses means across three head-training seeds. Seeds quantify optimization variability on a fixed dataset, not uncertainty over new traffic scenes.
  • Per-frame GT instances can repeat the same actor over time. Counts are not independent events or tracks.
  • The top-K budget uses validation GT counts for descriptive sensitivity analysis. It is not a prospective score threshold.
  • A GT captured by the phrase head but not Stage 5 is a prediction complementarity, not proof of a causal semantic mechanism.
  • Examples were selected deterministically from pronounced rank-gap cases, with at most three source videos per class/seed/outcome in the full archive. This page embeds representative cases from those groups.
  • No statistical-significance, zero-shot, open-vocabulary, or deployable-oracle claim is made.
